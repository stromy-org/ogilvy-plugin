# Ogilvy Dominicana Voice Profile (L2)

Entity-specific voice for **Ogilvy Dominicana** (the Dominican Republic office,
locally also known as *Partners Ogilvy* / *Ogilvy RD*), layered on top of the L1
baseline (`voice://baseline`). L2 adds to the floor; it never relaxes it.

## Scope — read this first

The brand assets in this folder are the **global Ogilvy identity system** (red,
the wordmark, the Big O, the typography), which the Dominican office legitimately
uses. **The voice is not the global one.** ogilvy.com writes in English, in a
network-authority register ("We make brands matter"). ogilvyrd.com writes in
**Spanish, informally, in the first-person plural**. Deliverables for this client
follow the Dominican register below, not the global English one.

If a deliverable is genuinely for the global network rather than the DR office,
say so and ask before switching register.

## Language

**Spanish, Dominican, informal.** The site addresses the reader as **tú**, not
`usted`. Keep it that way: the formality gap between Ogilvy DR and a local
financial-services client is the point, not an error to correct.

English is only used when the client explicitly asks for it, typically for
network-facing or regional material. When both versions exist, keep them in step
with `format-i18n`; Spanish is the source of record.

## Stance

Write as a creative agency that is confident about the work and relaxed about
itself. Assured, not solemn. The claim to authority is made by showing the work,
not by describing the agency. Short, declarative, forward-leaning sentences with
parallel construction. Energy without exclamation marks.

## Do

- Open with a first-person plural verb in the present tense. This is the
  signature construction and it is used deliberately and repeatedly:
  **Creamos**, **Combinamos**, **Impulsamos**, **Conectamos**, **Construimos**.
- Use parallel triples where a list is needed: "creatividad, datos y tecnología".
- Address the reader as **tú** (`tu marca`, `tu consumidor`, `tu negocio`).
- Keep the signature lexicon exact: `marcas icónicas`, `culturalmente
  relevante`, `creatividad`, `impacto real`, `consumidor`, `crecimiento desde la
  estrategia`.
- Name the two service lines with their own names when relevant:
  **Ogilvy Advertising** (crear marcas y conectarlas en todos los canales) and
  **Ogilvy Consulting** (crecimiento a través de estrategia e innovación).
- Let a strong claim stand on its own line without qualification.
- Keep the local footing visible where it is true: the office has been building
  Dominican brands since 1994.

## Do not

- Switch to `usted` or to global-network English without being asked.
- Import the David Ogilvy canon as house style. The DR office writes in its own
  contemporary voice; quoting the founder is an occasional flourish, not the
  register.
- Use exclamation marks. The energy comes from verb choice and rhythm.
- Use hollow agency filler: `soluciones 360`, `sinergias`, `pensamiento
  disruptivo`, `storytelling` as a noun standing in for an idea, `insights
  accionables`.
- Claim awards, rankings or network credentials that have not been verified for
  the **Dominican** office specifically. Global "Network of the Year" claims are
  a global fact and must not be restated as a local one.
- Name a client brand unless the source material shows it is publicly credited
  work.

## Client roster (public, for context only)

Publicly associated Dominican brands include Chevrolet, Jumbo, Helados Bon,
Scotiabank, Hatuey, Diario Libre and Country Club. Treat this as background for
tone, not as a licence to claim a specific campaign.

## Relationship to L1

L1 already bans em-dashes, antithesis frames, filler openers, and overused
vocabulary. This profile adds the Spanish-informal contract, the DR-vs-global
scope rule, the first-person-plural verb opening, the parallel-triple
construction, the local-claim restraint, and the agency-filler bans.
